### About
---
Amiloride is a mild potassium-sparing diuretic medication used to treat high blood pressure, heart failure, and fluid retention (edema). It helps the body get rid of extra water and salt while preventing potassium loss.

### Mechanism of Action
---
#### Direct
**ENaC**
inhibition of the epithelial sodium channel ([[ENaC]]) in the kidney's late distal convoluted tubule and collecting duct of the nephron, this mechanism is independent of aldosterone.
#### Indirect
**Potassium Sparring & Other Ions Sparring**
Amiloride actions on [[ENaC]] will indirectly spar potassium decrease excretion K⁺ via [[ROMK1]]. [[ENaC]] blockade will eliminate the lumen negative electrical potential that Na⁺ reabsorption causes. This change in the electrical gradient inhibits the secretion potassium and other ions  H⁺, Ca²⁺, and Mg²⁺.

**[[RAAS]]**
Since [[ENaC]] itself is pharmacologically blocked, [[aldosterone]] can't fully act on its main target, so this compensatory hormonal surge is partially wasted aldosterone rises, but the channel it would normally activate is blocked, blunting the kidney's ability to respond to that hormonal signal.

**Uric acid**
Prolonged use of amiloride can reduce the excretion of renal uric acid by causing volume contraction and reabsorption of uric acid from the proximal convoluted tubule. Purely because of volume depletion driving more PCT reabsorption.
#### Pure Off Target
**[[NHEs]], especially [[NHE1]]**
Amiloride was actually the original tool compound used to identify NHE activity before more selective NHE inhibitors (like EIPA, cariporide) were developed. It blocks intracellular pH regulation and Na⁺-dependent proliferative signaling in many cell types.

**Acid Sensing Ion Channels**
Amiloride is the classic pharmacological blocker used to identify ASIC currents in neurons. This is actually one of its most cited off target uses experimentally, since ASICs are unrelated to renal Na⁺ handling but share some structural/pore similarity to ENaC.

**[[Na⁺ Ca²⁺ Exchanger]]**
Blocked at higher concentrations, relevant to some of amiloride's cardiac and vascular smooth muscle effects independent of diuresis.

**[[uPA]] inhibition** 
This is a genuine competitive inhibition at the enzyme's active site, not some indirect signaling effect. Amiloride competitively inhibits the catalytic activity of uPA, binding directly in uPA's specificity pocket (the S1 pocket, the same region that normally recognizes the enzyme's natural substrate).
