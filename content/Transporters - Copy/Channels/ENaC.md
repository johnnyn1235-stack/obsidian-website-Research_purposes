### About
---
ENaC stands for the epithelial sodium channel, a special protein pore in cell walls that lets pass Na⁺ through. It is found mainly in the kidneys, lungs, and colon, where it helps control salt and water levels in the body.

### Mechanism
---
**Activation**
Hormones: [[Aldosterone]], vasopressin
Protease Enzymes: Proteolytic Cleavage furin, prostasin, plasmin
Mechanical Stress: Fluid movement, physical forces over cells

**Apical Entry**
ENaC lets Na⁺ ions flow passively down their combined electrochemical gradient from the extracellular fluid or lumen into the epithelial cell. The inside of the cell has a negative electrical potential relative to the lumen, which actively pulls the positively charged sodium ions inward.

**Basolateral Removal** 
The Na+/K+-ATPase pumps the entered Na⁺ out across the basolateral membrane into the blood, using energy to maintain low intracellular sodium levels.

**Coupled Transport**
The inward movement of sodium via ENaC creates an osmotic and electrical environment that secondary transporters and water reabsorption rely upon.
