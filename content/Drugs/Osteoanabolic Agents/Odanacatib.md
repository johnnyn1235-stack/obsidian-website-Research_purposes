### About
---
Odanacatib is an investigational oral medication that was developed to treat postmenopausal osteoporosis by blocking cathepsin K, an enzyme that breaks down bone tissue. Although it proved effective at increasing bone density, its development was discontinued.

### Mechanism of Action
---
#### Direct
**Cathepsin K Inhibition**  
Odanacatib is a selective, potent and reversible inhibitor of cathepsin K (CatK), a lysosomal cysteine protease predominantly expressed in osteoclasts. Cathepsin K acts as the major collagenase responsible for degradation of type I collagen in the organic bone matrix during bone remodelling. Odanacatib → binds to and inhibits CatK in osteoclasts → osteoclasts cannot degrade demineralized collagen in the resorption lacunae → ↓ bone resorption. Unlike bisphosphonates or denosumab, odanacatib does not affect osteoclastogenesis or osteoclast survival.

#### Indirect
**Maintained osteoclast viability / Rebound resorption**  
ODN preserves osteoclast numbers (supported by sustained or ↑ [[TRAP5b]]). Upon withdrawal → abrupt restoration of CatK activity → rapid ↑ in short collagen resorption markers (sNTx, uNTx) → rebound bone loss and potential ↑ fracture risk during drug withdrawal.

**Uncoupled bone remodelling / ↑ BMD**  
↓ short/medium resorption markers (sNTx, uNTx) but only transient ↓ in sCTx, with accumulation of larger partially degraded fragments ([[sICTP]]). Bone formation markers (sPINP, sBSAP) show minimal early suppression and return near baseline over time → uncoupled remodelling (resorption ↓, formation relatively maintained) → net positive bone balance → progressive ↑ in lumbar spine and hip BMD.

**Off-target cerebrovascular risk**  
Off-target effects (mechanism incompletely defined; may involve CatK expression in vascular smooth muscle or off-target inhibition of other cathepsins) → ↑ incidence of cerebrovascular events (stroke) in the LOFT trial → primary reason for regulatory withdrawal, despite robust antifracture efficacy.
