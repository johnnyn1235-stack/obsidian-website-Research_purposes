### About
---
Amiloride is a mild potassium-sparing diuretic medication used to treat high blood pressure, heart failure, and fluid retention (edema). It helps the body get rid of extra water and salt while preventing potassium loss.

### Mechanism of Action
---
#### Direct
**ENaC**  
Amiloride directly inhibits the epithelial sodium channel ([[ENaC]]) in the late distal convoluted tubule, connecting tubule and collecting duct, reducing Na⁺ reabsorption and producing mild natriuresis. This mechanism is independent of aldosterone signalling, as amiloride blocks the channel directly regardless of circulating [[aldosterone]] levels.

#### Indirect
**Potassium Sparing**  
ENaC blockade reduces the lumen-negative transepithelial potential generated by Na⁺ reabsorption. This decreases secretion of K⁺ via [[ROMK]] and H⁺ by intercalated cells, producing potassium retention and a tendency toward metabolic acidosis. Magnesium excretion may also be modestly reduced.

**RAAS**  
Mild extracellular-volume contraction activates [[RAAS]] and increases aldosterone secretion. However, because ENaC is pharmacologically blocked, aldosterone cannot fully stimulate distal Na⁺ reabsorption, blunting the kidney's response to this compensatory hormonal signal.

**Uric Acid**  
Amiloride has little effect on uric-acid handling compared with thiazide or loop diuretics. Any reduction in urate excretion is generally mild and secondary to volume contraction rather than a direct effect on urate transport.

#### Pure Off Target
**NHEs (particularly NHE1)**  
Amiloride inhibits [[Na⁺/H⁺ exchangers]], particularly [[NHE1]], and was the original pharmacological tool used to characterize NHE activity before development of more selective inhibitors such as EIPA and cariporide.

**ASICs**  
At higher concentrations, amiloride blocks acid-sensing ion channels (ASICs), making it the classic experimental inhibitor of ASIC currents. This action is unrelated to its renal diuretic effects.

**Na⁺/Ca²⁺ Exchanger (NCX)**  
Amiloride inhibits the [[Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger]] at higher micromolar concentrations, contributing to experimental cardiac and vascular effects independent of ENaC.

**uPA**  
Amiloride directly and competitively inhibits urokinase-type plasminogen activator ([[uPA]]) by binding within its catalytic S1 specificity pocket. This off-target action has been studied in nephrotic syndrome and cancer biology because it reduces plasmin generation and downstream proteolytic signalling.
